COMMERCIAL & INDUSTRIAL

Planning an industrial RCC yard

Bring traffic patterns, loading needs and site constraints into the pavement conversation early.

Industrial and commercial yards serve different combinations of truck traffic, equipment, employees, loading and storage. A planning map that captures how the property operates helps the project team determine pavement limits and coordinate RCC with buildings, docks, utilities and existing surfaces.

Map the routes and loading areas

Show entrances, truck paths, turning areas, queueing, docks, containers, outdoor storage and emergency access. Note where heavy vehicles brake, turn in place or repeatedly cross the same area. Include equipment types and expected loads when available. These details help the pavement designer understand where demands vary across the site.

Review transitions and work sequencing

Identify connections to asphalt, concrete, gravel and building slabs. Pavement elevation and edge details affect drainage and access, so transition points should be part of the design discussion. If the facility will remain open, divide the work into practical phases and define temporary routes, delivery windows and protected work zones.

Coordinate utilities and drainage

Gather utility plans and mark drains, inlets, access covers, trenches and planned services. Confirm how surface runoff will move and where it will be managed. Existing water problems should be addressed in the project scope rather than hidden beneath a new pavement surface.

Build a complete project brief

Useful early information includes a current site plan, available soil reports, traffic and loading data, drainage concerns, utility locations, project limits, operating constraints and timing goals. A site visit can then focus on questions that drawings alone may not answer.

Final pavement design, base preparation, jointing and drainage require project-specific engineering and specifications.
